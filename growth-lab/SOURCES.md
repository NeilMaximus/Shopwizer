## The Apex Martech Matrix (research explorer)

Back on the page since 16 October 2026 (version 2.12, Neil), rebuilt in Growth Lab colours as the first three views of the research explorer (Your position, What it means, Your priorities; a carousel until version 2.13) and as WhatsApp images (`share/whatsapp/`). Version 2.4 had removed it.

- **Source.** MartechTribe's Apex Martech Matrix, developed with the CMO Council, retail industry example. Neil supplied three images on 16 October 2026: the matrix chart, the four company cards (Amazon, Zalando, Chalhoub Group, Bellavie with their Apex Scores) and an example Apex Martech Score output ("75/100, can increase to 87/100", with "Fix these areas" and "Doing great here"). MartechTribe's article: https://martechtribe.com/blog/there-is-no-best-in-class-technology-its-official (caption there: "The Apex Martech Matrix, developed by MartechTribe in collaboration with the CMO Council. Retail Industry example."). The network here blocks martechtribe.com, so the article was not re-read on 16 October 2026.
- **Data.** `config/apex-retail-example.json` holds every number the page shows. The 25 dot centres were measured from the chart image (plot area 1,154 x 1,066 px; single dots by colour detection, overlapping dots by eye, within about 2 px) and stored as percentages of the plot, with each retailer's quadrant taken from the chart's own colours and the dividers at 50.0 and 48.97 percent. Names come from the chart's numbered legend. Nothing is interpolated or invented: a dot that cannot be read is left out, never guessed.
- **Axes.** The page keeps MartechTribe's terms on the axes, "Martech presence" (up) and "Martech performance" (right), as on the chart, each with a plain meaning since version 2.13: "technology capabilities" and "ability to use the technology". The view's line gives the same meanings: the capabilities of the technology, and the organisation's ability to use them (MartechTribe's quadrant descriptions speak of functionality, and skills and processes). Neil approved the wording on 16 October 2026; MartechTribe to confirm (`PRELAUNCH.md`).
- **Highlighted retailers.** Since 16 October 2026 the chart highlights retailers South Africans know, at Neil's request: Amazon (Powerhouse), IKEA and Sainsbury’s (Overbuilt), H&M (Precision) and Fossil (Underbuilt). Each is a real dot from MartechTribe's chart in its own quadrant; only the position of its name label is chosen (`callout` in the data file). "IKEA" follows the company's own styling (the chart's legend reads "Ikea").
- **Earlier examples and scores.** The cards Neil supplied gave Apex Scores for four companies: Amazon 75/100, Zalando 50/100, Chalhoub Group 51/100 and Bellavie 29/100 (kept in the data file as `_source_cards`). The cards call Chalhoub Group Overbuilt and Zalando Precision, but the chart places them the other way round; the page follows the chart. Neither is highlighted now, and no score is shown on the position cards, because the newly highlighted retailers have no published score and none is carried over or invented. The one line meanings ("More technology than the team can use", "Broad technology, used well", "Behind on both", "Fewer tools, used very well") and each card's sentence are Growth Lab's plain language summaries of MartechTribe's cards.
- **Example score.** An illustration since 16 October 2026 (Neil): 63 today and 87 within reach, with Events, meetings and webinars +10 (maturity and features, shown as "Skills and tools both trail your peers"), Social media marketing +10 (maturity, "Tools in place, but underused") and Collaboration +4 (features, "Your team is ready, the tools are not"), adding up to the 24 point gap. Already strong: Affiliate marketing +0 and eCommerce marketing +0 (at or above the industry, shown as "Matching or beating your industry"), Video ads +1 (just above, "Just ahead of your industry"). It is MartechTribe's sample output (75 to 87, fixes +5, +5 and +2, kept in the data file as `scorecard._source`) moved down and scaled in proportion, so the page credits it as "Illustrative example, based on the Apex Martech Score by MartechTribe." and never as MartechTribe's output or a client's result. Maturity is MartechTribe's measure of skills, process and adoption (the team); features is its measure of functionality (the tools). The icons are Growth Lab's own line icons; no vendor logos.
- **Peers and the research base (version 2.13).** The page treats the 1,634 stacks as the wider research base and the relevant industry peers, selected for each client's business, as the client's comparison: "For your business, we select the relevant industry peers from the wider research base of 1,634 stacks." The score is described as benchmark alignment, not ROI or a revenue forecast. Scores: "Example starting score: 63/100" and "Potential score: 87/100"; the three priority improvements add 24 points (63 + 24 = 87). The areas already strong are shown without points, so the arithmetic stays with the three priorities.
- **Your next steps (version 2.13).** An illustrative plan written by Growth Lab, not MartechTribe data: the three example priorities in phases with plausible actions and role owners (Marketing operations, Social media lead, Head of marketing), then a progress review owned by Growth Lab with the client's team. No dates, results, savings or ROI. Credit: "Illustrative plan, based on the example priorities."
- **Position priorities (version 2.13).** Each position card says what the position could mean for a client's priorities (Neil, 16 October 2026), written by Growth Lab from MartechTribe's quadrant descriptions, and never advises buying more technology or moving to a bigger stack.
- **Credit lines on the page.** "Retail example of 25 retailers. Apex Martech Matrix by MartechTribe and the CMO Council." "Positions from the same retail example." "Illustrative example, based on the Apex Martech Score by MartechTribe."
- **Permission.** MartechTribe and the CMO Council must agree to the rebuilt example on Growth Lab's site and in WhatsApp images before release (`PRELAUNCH.md`). It is a retail industry example, never a Growth Lab client result, and is never presented as one.
